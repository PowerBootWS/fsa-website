What 2nd Class Power Engineering Actually Costs

Three buckets: what the regulator charges, what your study material costs, and what a rewrite costs. Most candidates budget for the first, underestimate the second, and never price the third at all.

2nd Class is six papers: 2A1, 2A2, 2A3, 2B1, 2B2 and 2B3. Each is 3 hours, 100 multiple-choice questions, and you need 65% to pass. Most working candidates take somewhere between eighteen months and three years to clear all six, and that timeline is the thing your budget has to survive.

Exam fees are set by your provincial regulator

Not by SOPEEC, and not by whoever sold you your study material. Every province publishes a fee schedule, and the numbers differ enough to matter. Two examples, taken from the published schedules rather than from hearsay:

Alberta (ABSA). The fee schedule states plainly: the fee payable for application and writing an examination is $113.10. Across six papers that is $678.60 in exam fees, assuming you pass each one first time. Annual registration of the certificate of competency is a separate $37.75.

British Columbia (Technical Safety BC). The Boilers, Pressure Vessels and Refrigeration fee schedule effective January 1, 2026 lists the Certification of Qualification exam for Second Class at $193 per exam written digitally in person, or $183 per exam written digitally and remotely, both plus GST. Six papers is $1,158 or $1,098. On top of that sit a one-time non-refundable Evaluation of Qualification at $130, and the granting of the certificate itself at $461 for 2nd Class. The same schedule already publishes the rates that take effect January 1, 2027, which rise to $203 and $192 per exam.

Check your own regulator, and check it again before you book. The figures above are Alberta and BC as published at the time of writing, and BC's own schedule shows the numbers moving next year. If you write in Saskatchewan, Ontario, Manitoba or Atlantic Canada, your regulator sets its own fees. Go to the source, not to a summary.

The rewrite is the cost nobody budgets

A failed paper is not a free retry. Technical Safety BC's fee schedule spells it out in a footnote: the exam fee applies to new examinations and re-examinations. In Alberta the exam fee is charged per application to write, which means the same thing in practice.

So a single failed paper costs you the fee again, plus another study cycle of weeks or months, plus the delay to the pay increase the certificate unlocks. In BC, one rewrite costs more than a month of most study subscriptions. That is the arithmetic that should drive your prep decision, and it almost never does.

Put another way: prep is not the expensive part of this process. Failing is.

Study material: three pricing models, three very different totals

Almost everything sold to 2nd Class candidates falls into one of three shapes. The sticker price tells you very little until you know which shape you are looking at.

Per-paper, one-time purchase

You buy a course for 2A1, then another for 2A2, and so on. Simple to understand and it does not run while you are not studying. The catch is the multiplier: whatever the per-paper price is, multiply it by six. Nothing you buy for paper five helps you while you are stuck on paper two, and a paper you find harder than expected does not cost extra, but it does mean money sitting idle.

Your total: per-paper price × 6.

Per-part subscription, billed monthly or bi-weekly

You subscribe to the material for one exam part and pay until you cancel. This looks like the cheapest option on the checkout page, and it has two multipliers rather than one. It multiplies by the number of parts you subscribe to at once, and it multiplies by how long you take. A rate that looks modest for two weeks on one part behaves very differently when you are studying two parts across eight months.

Your total: rate per part × number of parts × number of billing periods you stay subscribed.

Flat, all-inclusive subscription

One price for every paper in the class. It runs whether you study that month or not, so a month you skip is a month wasted. In exchange it has no multipliers: studying two papers at once costs the same as one, and a paper that takes twice as long as planned does not change the rate.

Your total: monthly price × number of months, regardless of how many papers you are working on.

Work out your own number

Four steps, ten minutes, and it will change which option looks cheapest:

  1. Write down your real timeline. Not the optimistic one. How many months will you realistically be studying, at your shift pattern, for all six papers?
  2. Write down how many papers you will have in flight at once. Candidates who study a paper at a time and candidates who run two in parallel get completely different answers from the same price list.
  3. Apply the multipliers above to whatever you are considering, and get to a single number for the full certificate rather than a monthly one.
  4. Add your regulator's exam fees, plus one rewrite. If you would not have budgeted for that rewrite, that is the point.
