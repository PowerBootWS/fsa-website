The Free PanGlobal Textbooks: What Each One Covers, and What They Do Not Do

The power engineering books everyone studies from are free now. Here is which class needs which ones, what each unit actually covers, and the honest limit of a textbook on its own.

If you have been searching for the PanGlobal textbooks and keep landing on dead links or forum threads asking where to find a PDF, here is the short version: PanGlobal Training Systems, the company, ceased operations in 2025. The books did not disappear with it, and everyone in the trade still calls them the PanGlobal textbooks. Ownership of the Power Engineering Learning Materials passed jointly to NAIT, SAIT, and BCIT, who now publish the complete set to the public, free, under a Creative Commons licence. No login, no purchase, no waiting for a reprint.

That is genuinely good news, and it is also confusing if you do not know where the books ended up or which edition your class needs. This guide covers both: what happened, what the licence lets you do with the files, and a class-by-class map of what each book actually teaches, using the same descriptions as the download page itself. It closes with the part most free-resource roundups skip: what a textbook does not do, and why that matters before your exam date, not after it.

What Happened to PanGlobal, and Who Holds the Books Now

For decades, PanGlobal Training Systems published the standard learning materials Canadian power engineering candidates studied from at every class. When PanGlobal ceased operations in 2025, there was a real window where those books were at risk of just quietly going stale, sitting behind a defunct storefront while candidates kept writing exams against a syllabus the materials were built for.

That did not happen. Ownership of the Power Engineering Learning Materials passed jointly to three of the technical institutes that had been involved in developing them: the Northern Alberta Institute of Technology (NAIT), the Southern Alberta Institute of Technology (SAIT), and the British Columbia Institute of Technology (BCIT). Rather than sell the rights on or let the content lapse, the three institutes released the entire set to the public under a Creative Commons licence. The content itself was not rewritten in the handover; it is the same SOPEEC-aligned material, now stewarded by three public institutions instead of one private vendor.

PanGlobal no longer exists as a vendor, but the name stuck to the books, and it is common in the trade to still call them the PanGlobal textbooks. There is no PanGlobal storefront to buy from and no PanGlobal account to create, so you never need to pay for a digital copy; the official PDFs are free from NAIT, SAIT, and BCIT. A used physical copy is a different thing, and it is entirely normal to see a power engineer selling their own PanGlobal textbooks once they have passed.

What the Licence Actually Means for You

The books are released under Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0 International, usually written as CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. In plain terms, broken into its three parts:

  • Attribution. You credit NAIT, SAIT, and BCIT as the source when you share or adapt the material. The credit line is printed in the preface of each book.
  • NonCommercial. You cannot sell the books or charge for access to them. Downloading, reading, and studying from them is unaffected; the restriction is on turning them into a paid product.
  • ShareAlike. If you adapt or build on the material, whatever you produce has to carry the same licence forward, not a more restrictive one.

This is not legal advice, and the full terms are worth a read on their own if you plan to redistribute or adapt anything beyond personal study. For the ordinary case, a candidate downloading a PDF to study from, printing a chapter, or passing a copy to a coworker sitting the same exam, none of this is a barrier. It exists to keep the books free and to stop someone repackaging public materials as a paid course without attribution, not to gate normal studying.

The Complete Set, by Edition

Each class has its own edition number, and it is worth knowing yours, because searching for a specific edition is often faster than searching by class name alone. The current editions are:

  • 4th Class: edition E35. 12 books for the A papers, 12 for the B papers. 24 units total.
  • 3rd Class: edition E30. 2 books for the A side (3A1, 3A2), 2 for the B side (3B1, 3B2). 4 books total.
  • 2nd Class: edition E25. 3 books for the A side (2A1, 2A2, 2A3), 3 for the B side (2B1, 2B2, 2B3). 6 books total.

All three sets are the current editions, aligned with the SOPEEC syllabus. If a copy you find elsewhere is labelled with an older edition code, it may predate a syllabus update; the set at the library is kept current.

What's in the 4th Class Set (E35)

4th Class is two exam papers, 4A and 4B, sold and written separately, and each paper draws on twelve short topic units rather than one long book. For a full breakdown of how the papers themselves are structured, see the 4th Class exam guide.

Part A, twelve units, covers the theory and safety foundation: elementary mechanics and dynamics, physical, chemical, and thermodynamic principles, an introduction to power engineering and its governance in Canada, plant and fire safety, plant operations and the environment, material science and welding, fluid handling, basic electrotechnology, instrumentation and controls, industrial communication skills, and an introduction to boiler designs and boiler systems.

Part B, another twelve units, moves into equipment and plant operation: lubrication and bearings, pumps and compressors, boiler safety devices, boiler plant operation and management, plant maintenance, water treatment, prime movers and heat engines, plant auxiliary systems, compression and absorption refrigeration, HVAC fundamentals, building environmental systems, and typical industrial plant configurations.

Between the two papers, that is the full scope of entry-level plant knowledge, from basic mechanics through to the equipment you will actually be standing in front of on shift.

What's in the 3rd Class Set (E30)

3rd Class steps up to four papers, and the book count matches one for one. Scope details for each paper are in the 3rd Class exam guide.

  • 3A1: Mathematics, Applied Science & Drawing. Mathematics, heat and steam properties, calorimetry, metallurgy and materials, and industrial drawings. This is the heaviest calculation load of the four papers.
  • 3A2: Legislation, Codes & Plant Administration. Legislation and codes for power engineers, plant administration, and related operational fundamentals.
  • 3B1: Boilers & Steam Generation. Watertube and firetube boiler designs, water circulation, boiler fittings, mountings, and controls.
  • 3B2: Prime Movers & Rotating Equipment. Steam turbine principles and design, engines, and other prime movers and rotating plant equipment.

What's in the 2nd Class Set (E25)

2nd Class is six papers, three under A and three under B, and it is the widest single certificate on this site: everything from applied mechanics through to electrotechnology and refrigeration. Full paper-by-paper detail is in the 2nd Class exam guide.

  • 2A1: Applied Mechanics, ASME Code & Administration. Applied mechanics and strength of materials, ASME Code calculations, and industrial administration.
  • 2A2: Thermodynamics, Metallurgy & Materials. Applied thermodynamics and steam cycles, metallurgy, heat treatment, welding, and materials testing.
  • 2A3: Boilers, Pumps & Water Treatment. Boiler design, construction and operation, pumps, and feedwater and internal boiler-water treatment.
  • 2B1: Heat Engines, Lubrication, Piping & Drawing. Steam turbines, internal-combustion and gas turbines, lubrication, piping, and mechanical drawing.
  • 2B2: Plant Systems, Instrumentation, Fuels & Environment. Feedwater and steam systems, control instrumentation, combustion and fuels, and environmental equipment.
  • 2B3: Electrotechnology, Compression & Refrigeration. AC/DC theory, generators, motors, transformers, protection, compression, and industrial refrigeration.

What a Free Textbook Does, and What It Does Not Do

Say the honest thing plainly: these are the right books. They are the same current, SOPEEC-aligned content the exams are written against, not a summary or a third-party guess at the syllabus, and they cost nothing. If you are deciding whether to trust free material over a paid alternative, that question does not really apply here, the content itself is not in question.

What is worth being straight about is what a 3,000-page PDF set is not built to do.

It does not tell you what to read first. Every 2nd Class candidate opens six books at once with no sense of which paper carries the most marks for the time it takes, or which topics show up on the exam constantly versus once in a while. Reading in book order, cover to cover, is rarely the fastest route to a pass.

It does not tell you whether you are ready. Every SOPEEC paper from 5th through 2nd Class is 100 multiple-choice questions, 65% to pass, in roughly three to three and a half hours depending on jurisdiction. A textbook has no mechanism for putting you under that exact format and timing before exam day, and no way to tell you that you understand a topic in the abstract but freeze on it under a clock.

It does not tell you why you got a question wrong. This is the one candidates feel most, usually at eleven at night after a twelve-hour shift, working through a practice question with no explanation attached. A PDF cannot answer "why is that wrong" back. Structured practice with feedback attached is what turns reading into something you can recall under pressure, not just something you have seen once.

And it does not sequence itself around your schedule. Working operators are not studying full time. A textbook has no opinion on whether you should tackle your hardest paper first while you have energy for it, or build momentum on your strongest paper before circling back, and it has no memory of what you covered last week versus what you have forgotten since.

None of that is a knock on the books. It is the difference between content and a study system, and it is worth knowing before you are three weeks from an exam date wondering why hours of reading have not translated into confidence on practice questions. Use the free books. Then find out where you actually stand. Reading tells you what to study. It does not tell you whether you would pass if the exam were today. Past Papers Are a Different Resource

Textbooks and past papers get confused often enough that it is worth separating them. The books above teach the content. Past papers, where they exist and are genuinely current, show you the format questions are asked in. They serve different purposes and neither substitutes for the other; see past papers for power engineering exams for what is actually available and what is not worth chasing.
