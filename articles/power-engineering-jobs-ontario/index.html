Power Engineering Jobs in Ontario

Ontario advertises fewer power engineering jobs than Alberta and more senior ones. For anyone holding or chasing a 2nd Class ticket, it is the strongest market in Canada, and the raw posting count hides it.

Ontario posted 254 certification-naming power engineering jobs in the three months we measured, second to Alberta's 480. On volume alone it looks like the runner-up market.

It isn't. Ontario advertised more senior roles than Alberta did — 89 postings naming 2nd or 1st Class against Alberta's 71 — on roughly half the total volume. If you hold a 2nd Class ticket or are working toward one, Ontario is the strongest market in the country for you, and its total posting count actively hides that.

Where these numbers come from. Full Steam Ahead runs a Canadian power engineering job board, and this article is built from what it indexed over the three months from 8 May to 8 August 2026: 1,351 postings across Canada that actually named a power engineering certification. Postings that named no certificate class were excluded, so general maintenance and building-operator roles are not padding these counts. Two honest limits: this is what one board captured, not the whole market, and almost no Canadian employer publishes a wage in the posting, which is why the pay figures come from Job Bank rather than from us.

That last row is not a rounding error, it is a finding. More than a third of Ontario's postings came from employers who posted once or twice and belong to no obvious group: a tire plant, an ice cream maker, a salt mine, a cannabis producer, a transit commission, a waste handler, a chocolate manufacturer, a dairy, a gold mine, an art gallery. Ontario runs on a very long tail of industrial and institutional plants, each needing a certified operator or two.

Two rows deserve a second look:

Food and beverage processing is the biggest industrial employer of power engineers in Ontario, ahead of steel and auto. Maple Leaf Foods alone posted 11 roles in three months, more than any other employer in the province. Thirteen of those 33 food-sector postings wanted 2nd or 1st Class. If you picture Ontario power engineering as steel mills and nuclear, you are picturing the smaller half of it.

Nearly every posting from a staffing agency was a senior role — 14 of 15. That is a hiring-channel fact, not a coincidence, and it changes how you should look for work. See below.

What the jobs are called

Job titles in this trade are inconsistent, and searching for the wrong one hides half the market. Across Ontario postings:

Patterns overlap, since one posting can be a "Chief Stationary Engineer". The practical point is that fewer than half of Ontario's power engineering jobs have "engineer" in the title in the way you would search for it. Real examples from the period: Thermal Operating Technician, Utility Operations Manager, Process Operator, Supervisor - Utilities and Yard Operations, Production Supervisor, 2nd Class TSSA, Powerhouse Stationary Engineer/Operator, Assistant Shift Engineer. If you search only "power engineer", you are missing roughly six in ten postings.

The GTA has the most jobs and the lowest senior share. That is what a market full of commercial buildings, hospitals and campuses looks like.

Northern Ontario is the mirror image: 23 postings, 14 of them senior. Thunder Bay, Chapleau, Kapuskasing and the mining and paper towns advertise less often and, when they do, they are usually looking for someone with a 2nd or 1st Class ticket. Eastern Ontario at 46% senior sits in between, carried by Ottawa institutions, Chalk River and the Trenton and Belleville corridor.

Top individual cities: Toronto 46, Hamilton 21, Ottawa 18, Windsor 16, London 14, Nanticoke 8, Chapleau 8, Scarborough 7, Thunder Bay 7, Mississauga 7.

Compare that with Alberta, where two health authorities and one facilities contractor account for the top of the list. Ontario's leaderboard runs from meat processing to steel to auto to forestry to district energy to a university, and the largest poster in the province managed 11 roles in three months. There is no single employer here worth building a job search around.

How often the jobs appear

Ontario's postings arrived steadily rather than in waves: 73 in May, 87 in June, 79 in July, with August partially counted. That is roughly 18 to 20% of national volume every month, without the turnaround-driven surges that make Alberta lumpy. For anyone job hunting on a schedule, a steady market is easier to plan around than a bigger, spikier one.

What it pays

Job Bank's wage data for power engineers, updated 19 November 2025 from Statistics Canada's Labour Force Survey for 2023 to 2024, puts Ontario at a median of $57.00/hour, with a low of $33.37 and the highest ceiling in Canada at $87.00. Alberta's median is marginally higher at $57.69, but no province tops Ontario's high end.

That ceiling and this certificate mix are the same fact seen twice. Job Bank does not split wages by certificate class, so a $33 floor and an $87 ceiling in one province mostly describes the distance between the bottom and the top of the certification ladder. Ontario advertises more of the top of that ladder than anywhere else in the country. Full national comparison: power engineer salary in Canada.

What this means if you are upgrading

  • The 3rd to 2nd Class step pays off here more than anywhere else in Canada. 76 Ontario postings named 2nd Class in three months, more than any other province in absolute terms and nearly three times Ontario's share of national posting volume.
  • Get on recruiters' desks. Fourteen of the 15 agency postings were senior roles. In Ontario, a meaningful slice of the 2nd Class market never appears on a company careers page at all.
  • Search by what the job does, not by "power engineer". Six in ten postings use a different title. Operator, technician, shift engineer, utilities supervisor.
  • If you want senior work, look north and east. Northern Ontario postings were 61% senior, Eastern 46%. The GTA has the volume; the regions have the tickets.
  • Do not write off food processing. It is the largest industrial employer of power engineers in the province and it wanted 2nd or 1st Class in 13 of 33 postings.

If you are working toward the ticket, start with the six SOPEEC 2nd Class papers. Ontario exams are administered by TSSA, and the SOPEEC syllabus is the same one used across the country, so material prepared for any province applies.
