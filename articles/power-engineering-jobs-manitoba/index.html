Power Engineering Jobs in Manitoba

Half of Manitoba's power engineering postings name 5th Class, the highest share in Canada. It is the easiest province to break into and one of the thinnest to top out in.

Manitoba posted 100 certification-naming power engineering jobs in the three months we measured, fourth in Canada behind Alberta, BC and Ontario, and ahead of both Saskatchewan and Nova Scotia. For a province of its size that is a lot of postings.

It is also the most distinctive market in the country, for one reason: Manitoba is where the entry-level work is.

Where these numbers come from. Full Steam Ahead runs a Canadian power engineering job board, and this article is built from what it indexed over the three months from 8 May to 8 August 2026: 1,351 postings across Canada that actually named a power engineering certification. Postings that named no certificate class were excluded, so general maintenance and building-operator roles are not padding these counts. Two honest limits: this is what one board captured, not the whole market, and almost no Canadian employer publishes a wage in the posting, which is why the pay figures come from Job Bank rather than from us.

Postings often name more than one class, so these overlap. Nearly half of Manitoba's postings name 5th Class, the highest share of any province in Canada. Alberta's figure is 31%, BC's 23%, Ontario's 5%, and Nova Scotia's is zero.

Nearly half of Manitoba's power engineering demand comes from the health system. Southern Health-Santé Sud alone posted 20 roles in three months, the Winnipeg Regional Health Authority 10, Prairie Mountain Health nine, Shared Health three. Add the school divisions, the universities and the Province of Manitoba and the public sector accounts for roughly two-thirds of the province's advertised jobs.

Those public-sector roles are almost entirely 5th and 4th Class. Of the 45 health-sector postings, two named a senior ticket.

Where the senior work actually is

Manitoba's 2nd and 1st Class demand does not come from the institutions. It comes from a short list of private industrial plants, and they are mostly not in Winnipeg:

  • Roquette posted three roles, all of them 2nd Class, at its plant in Portage la Prairie.
  • Diageo posted two, both for a First Class Chief Power Engineer, at Gimli.
  • Canadian Kraft Paper posted a Steam Chief role in The Pas.
  • Black & McDonald posted a 2nd Class Shift Engineer.
  • Chemtrade posted five roles, and the industrial tail also includes Winpak, Bunge, Bayer, Cascades, Greif and Linde.

That is the whole senior market for a three-month window. It is small enough to watch by name, which is genuinely useful information: in Manitoba you can track every employer likely to want a 2nd Class ticket on a single page.

Winnipeg is only 37% of the province's postings, the lowest big-city concentration of any province we have looked at. Steinbach at 17 postings is the story here, along with Winkler at seven and Killarney at five: that is Southern Health-Santé Sud staffing hospitals and personal care homes across the south of the province. If you are prepared to work outside Winnipeg, Manitoba's entry-level market is wide open.

What the jobs are called

Manitoba is comparatively honest with titles. Just over half of postings, 51%, say stationary engineer, power engineer or operating engineer outright. Another 13% are supervisor, manager or superintendent roles, 11% are operator roles and 7% are building or facility operator titles. Only 5% use chief, shift or lead engineer wording, which is consistent with a market this weighted toward entry level.

How often the jobs appear

Manitoba's postings arrive in batches rather than a steady stream: 42 in May, 19 in June, 32 in July. That is what a market dominated by a handful of large public employers looks like. When a health authority runs a hiring round, the month spikes; when it doesn't, the province goes quiet. Worth knowing if you are timing a search.

What it pays

Job Bank's wage data for power engineers, updated 19 November 2025 from Statistics Canada's Labour Force Survey for 2023 to 2024, puts Manitoba at a median of $39.50/hour, with a low of $29.00 and a high of $50.48.

The median sits below the national $49.23, and the ceiling is the second-lowest in the country, above only Prince Edward Island and Nova Scotia. Manitoba has one of the narrowest wage ranges in Canada, spanning about $21 an hour from low to high, against Ontario's $54.

That narrowness is the same fact as the certificate mix. Job Bank does not split wages by certificate class, and a market made mostly of 5th and 4th Class institutional roles does not generate the top of the range that heavy industry does. Full comparison: power engineer salary in Canada.

What this means if you are upgrading

  • Manitoba is an excellent place to start and a hard place to top out. Getting a first ticket and a first job is easier here than almost anywhere. The ceiling arrives sooner.
  • Look outside Winnipeg for entry work. Steinbach, Winkler, Brandon and Killarney are where the health authority volume is.
  • Look at private plants for senior work, and there are few enough to name: Roquette, Diageo, Canadian Kraft Paper, Chemtrade, Winpak, Bunge.
  • Plan for the ticket to travel. Certification is interprovincial. A Manitoba 2nd Class holder facing 11 senior postings a quarter at home is two provinces away from Ontario's 89.
