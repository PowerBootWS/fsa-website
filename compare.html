Drilling Only Works on Something You Already Know

Practice questions are genuinely valuable, and you should be doing them. The question is what they're doing for you.

A Bank Measures. It Doesn't Teach.

Drilling questions tells you what you don't know. That is useful and it is where a lot of candidates should start. But when the readout says you're weak on thermodynamics, a bank hands you more thermodynamics questions to get wrong. Something still has to close the gap.

The common frustration
"I've done a thousand questions and my score on that unit hasn't moved."
An Explanation Answers the Question You Just Missed

A worked explanation tells you why B was right. It does not answer "then why doesn't that apply on the other one" or "where did that formula come from", which is the question you actually have at 11pm. That needs something you can talk back to.

Questions Written to a Syllabus Aren't Questions Written to an Exam

The published objectives tell you the topic. They do not tell you the depth, the phrasing, or which of four plausible answers a marker is actually separating. That comes from having sat the paper, taught it, and marked it, which is the part you cannot generate from a textbook.

What It Costs Over the Time You'll Really Study

A monthly subscription looks worse than a one-time price on a spreadsheet that assumes you stay subscribed for a year. Most people don't, and the design of the thing assumes you won't.

You subscribe for the months you're actually studying. Cancel the week you sit the paper and come back when you start the next one. Your progress is saved, you don't repurchase, and there's no access window closing behind you. A lot of our students subscribe for a month or two, write, and cancel. That is the product working as intended, not a loophole.

Against a tutorial course the arithmetic isn't close. Four 3rd Class papers at around $1,400 each is roughly $5,600. The same four papers here, with lessons, coaching and a tutor, is $99 a month for however many months you're actually at it.

Against a one-time question bank, you're comparing two different things. A bank is cheaper than a course and always will be, because it isn't one. If all you need is drilling, buy the drilling. If you need to be taught, the cheaper thing doesn't do the job.

And for 4th Class we don't bill monthly at all: it's $99 for the paper, charged once, covering a full year, and it never renews.

Where Something Else Is the Better Buy

We'd rather you spent your money on the right thing than on us.

You're writing 5th Class, 1st Class, or a refrigeration ticket

We don't sell those. We cover 4th, 3rd and 2nd Class and nothing else. A tool with a broader ladder, like SteamTicket, or an established provider like Power Engineering 101, will serve you where we can't.

You need an ABSA-accepted course to be allowed to sit 4th Class

Then you need an accepted course, and we are not one. ABSA requires approved coursework before you write. Power Engineering 101 has 4th Class courses ABSA has accepted. What we sell for 4th Class is exam prep to run alongside the program you're already enrolled in.

You already know the material and just want reps

If you've done the course, you understand the theory, and you want volume before exam day, a question bank is the cheaper and more direct answer. Buy the bank. Our free practice exam will tell you honestly whether that's where you are.

You want to study offline with no signal

We're a browser platform and need a connection. If you study in a plant basement with no data, an app that works offline is a real advantage and we won't pretend otherwise.

Common Questions

Is this comparison fair?

It's built from each provider's own public pages, checked on 14 September 2026, and it says plainly where they beat us. Prices and features change, so confirm current details on their site before you decide. If we've got something wrong, email support@fullsteamahead.ca and we'll correct it.

Why does it matter who wrote the questions?

Because the syllabus tells you the topic and not the depth. Two questions can cite the same objective and test completely different things, and only one of them resembles what shows up on the paper. Knowing which is which comes from having sat, taught and marked these exams.

Can I use a question bank and Full Steam Ahead together?

Of course, and plenty do. Learn it here, then drill it anywhere you like. Our own practice exams are included and adaptive, so most people find they don't need a second source, but nothing stops you.

What happens if I cancel?

Billing stops, your access runs to the end of the period you paid for, and your progress is saved. Come back whenever you start the next paper and pick up where you left off. There's no repurchase and no reactivation fee.

Are the free textbooks really free?

Yes. When PanGlobal wound down, the learning materials passed to NAIT, SAIT and BCIT, who released them under a Creative Commons licence. We host the full set, ungated, at our library. Anyone telling you those books are theirs to sell is wrong.

Which should I buy if I'm not sure?

Sit the free practice exam first. If your gaps are scattered across topics you've never properly studied, you need teaching. If they're concentrated and you mostly know the material, you need reps. The report tells you which, and it costs nothing either way.
